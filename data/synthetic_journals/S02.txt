w1.1: I am really excited about this course, mostly because of the international part. I am Italian and I have always found it easy to talk to people from anywhere, so I suspect this will be the easiest element of the whole semester for me.

w1.2: My own culture is quite expressive and we interrupt each other constantly, which people from elsewhere sometimes read as arguing. It is not arguing. Knowing that about myself at least lets me explain it when somebody looks alarmed.

w1.3: I had never seen a Circos plot before this week and I remain unconvinced that they are the clearest way to present structural variants.

w4.1: Finding a time slot that worked for both sites took us four days of messages for a thirty minute meeting. I understand there is only one hour of difference between us, but somehow it was still a nightmare.

w4.2: There are only three students left in Zurich, which makes the whole COIL idea feel rather thin. In our group of six we are five from Utrecht and one from Zurich, so in practice it is us plus a guest.

w7.1: I have stopped thinking of the international element as a real part of the course. We do the joint session because it is on the schedule, and afterwards everyone goes back to working with whoever is sitting next to them.

w7.2: The paper discussions themselves are good. Reading a methods section properly and then arguing about whether the controls were adequate is the most useful thing I have done this semester.

w10.1: If I am honest I would not choose the COIL version of this course again. Not because international collaboration is a bad idea, but because doing it badly is worse than not doing it at all.

w10.2: I also have to admit that I am impatient with people who do not answer messages within a day, and that is probably as much a cultural thing on my side as on theirs. I am not certain I gave them a fair chance.
