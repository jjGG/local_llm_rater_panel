Reflection journal — applied statistics seminar

Week 1
• What do you want to get out of this seminar?
I want to be able to read a mixed model output without guessing. The seminar is online, which suits me perfectly. We are apparently going to be paired with students from the partner university, and I do not really see what that adds.

• How do you learn best?
Alone, with a textbook. Group work usually slows me down.

Week 4
• How is the joint work going?
Slowly. Our two groups were taught different notation for the same quantity, which cost us an entire session before anyone noticed. I now write out what I mean instead of assuming the symbol is universal. The problem sets themselves are well designed.

• Anything else worth noting?
The recordings are useful. I would prefer fewer breakout rooms.

Week 7
• Has your view of the joint element changed?
Somewhat. I still think it costs time, but the two groups make different mistakes, and checking my work against theirs has caught two of mine. I also know now that the legal framework they work under for sharing data is not the same as ours, which I had not considered at all before.

• Anything difficult?
Time zones, mostly. I am someone who reads a slow reply as disinterest, which says more about me than about them.

Week 10
• Final thoughts on the seminar?
The statistics content was excellent and I would take it again. The joint element I have come round to, largely because it forced me to explain myself to people who did not share my assumptions. I still dislike breakout rooms.

• Advice for next year?
Give the two groups a shared glossary in week one. It would have saved us a fortnight.
