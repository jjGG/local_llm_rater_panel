Reflection journal — chamber ensemble project

Week 1
• Why did you join the ensemble project?
I wanted to play chamber music with people who are better than me. The rehearsal room is freezing and the piano is a disgrace. I am genuinely excited that half the group is coming from abroad, because I have only ever played with musicians trained the way I was.

• How would you rate your own playing?
Technically fine, musically cautious. I have been told more than once that I follow rather than lead.

Week 4
• How are rehearsals going?
We spent two hours on eight bars, which was maddening and probably necessary. What I did not expect is that their tradition treats rubato as a starting point rather than a decision, and I have had to learn what they mean by it. The parts arrived with the wrong transposition.

• Has there been any friction?
Yes. Our first violinist and their cellist disagree about everything, and some of it is genuinely about how one is supposed to behave in a rehearsal rather than about the music. The room booking system has failed twice as well.

Week 7
• Has anything shifted in how you play or rehearse?
I have started taking my own entries instead of waiting to be led, which is not something I would have done in September. I have also stopped apologising before every suggestion, because it was being read as a lack of conviction rather than as politeness. The concert programme is finally decided.

• Any regrets?
I have learned to ask what someone means rather than guessing, and it took me far too long. The catering at the dress rehearsal was excellent.

Week 10
• What did the project give you?
A much better ear. Also the discovery that I am more rigid about how music ought to be rehearsed than I ever thought I was. I would like to keep playing with two of them after this.

• Anything the organisers should change?
Heat the rehearsal room.
