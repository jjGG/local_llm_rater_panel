Reflection journal — design studio

Week 1
• What are you hoping to get out of this studio?
I chose this studio mainly because of the site. The old harbour district is the most interesting piece of the city and I have wanted to work on it since second year. I am also quite curious about working with the group at the partner school, because I have never designed anything with people I cannot meet in person. The reading pack is enormous.

• How do you usually work in a team?
I like to sketch first and talk later. That has annoyed people before. My model-making is slow but I get there in the end.

Week 4
• How is the collaboration developing?
We had our first shared review on Tuesday. I have learned that their school treats a review as a debate rather than a presentation, which is completely different from how we do it. I enjoyed it far more than I expected to. The file sharing is a disaster and we have lost work twice.

• Has anything been frustrating?
The time difference means our joint calls are always at the end of my day. I am not at my best then, and I am a blunt person, which probably lands differently with a group I do not know well. The plotter queue is also permanently full.

Week 7
• What has changed in how you work?
I now write a short agenda before every joint call. I have stopped using slang in them, because half of it does not survive translation. The concrete casting workshop was the highlight of the semester.

• Any low points?
Our scheme was criticised quite hard in the last review. Part of that was because the two groups never agreed on what the word public meant for this site, and it cost us. The canteen has stopped doing the good soup.

Week 10
• Looking back, what did you learn?
The studio taught me more about drawing than any course so far. I am much less nervous now about working with people whose training is different from mine. I would still have liked to meet them in person.

• Would you recommend it to next year's students?
Yes, but fix the file sharing first. And schedule the joint calls at a civilised hour.
