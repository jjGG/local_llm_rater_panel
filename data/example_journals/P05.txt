w1.1: I am more excited about this ensemble than about anything else I am taking this term, mostly because of the visiting players. I was raised in a household where you did not contradict the person holding the baton, and then trained somewhere you were marked down for failing to argue with the conductor. I have spent years translating between those two instincts, so a rehearsal room full of people trained differently feels less like a problem and more like an ordinary Tuesday.

w1.2: We are opening with a piece I have played before, which I had assumed would make the first rehearsal easy. It made it harder, because I had to unlearn a tempo I was attached to.

w4.1: When the visiting cellist went quiet after my suggestion about the phrasing, my first assumption was that she disagreed with me. Where I trained, silence after a proposal means it is being taken seriously. I had to stop myself from explaining the same point three more times.

w4.2: The rehearsal load was not shared fairly this block. Two of us prepared the difficult movement and four of us prepared the easy one, and I was one of the two.

w7.1: The best moment so far was realising that the visiting violinist and I had been given contradictory instructions by our two teachers, and simply deciding between ourselves which one to follow. That felt like an ensemble rather than a timetabled activity.

w7.2: I apologise before I ask a question. I have been told it makes me sound unsure of a reading I am actually quite confident about. It comes from home and I doubt it is going anywhere.

w10.1: There was a real disagreement in week nine that we never resolved. One player stopped replying to messages and we performed without them. Nobody handled it well, and I include myself in that.

w10.2: Looking back I was completely won over by the end, which surprises me because I began the term assuming the visiting players were a scheduling gimmick. What changed my mind was not a concert but a forty minute argument about a repeat.

w10.3: I understand now that I hear directness as rudeness a good deal faster than the people I trained alongside do, and that this is something I brought into the room rather than anything anyone did to me.
