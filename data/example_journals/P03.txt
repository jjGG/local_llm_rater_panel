w1.1: I grew up between two languages and two sets of grandparents who disagreed about almost everything, so a studio group with people from four countries feels ordinary to me rather than daunting. I already know that the same building gets described completely differently depending on who is doing the describing.

w1.2: The first assignment is a measured drawing of a facade of our own choosing. I badly underestimated how long that takes to do properly and will start earlier next time.

w4.1: Our studio group divided the survey work by building rather than by task, which meant everyone had to learn the whole method instead of becoming the person who only ever draws sections. It was slower and much better.

w4.2: I have always been someone who switches into a far more formal register the moment I am not speaking my first language, and it happens whether I intend it or not. People occasionally read it as coldness.

w7.1: I learned that what I was taught to call a courtyard covers at least three different things depending on which tradition you were trained in. Having that pointed out by somebody trained differently was more useful than any glossary.

w7.2: Studio was fine.

w10.1: I would take this studio again and I have already told the second years to choose it. The mixed groups are the reason for that, not a side effect of it.

w10.2: I am not a confident presenter and ten weeks has not changed that. But I noticed I was less anxious presenting to the mixed group than to my own cohort, which I had not expected at all.
