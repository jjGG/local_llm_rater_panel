w1.1: I signed up for this seminar mainly because it is taught jointly with the group at the partner institution, and I have wanted an excuse to work with people outside my own department for two years now. I come from a family where everyone talks over everyone else at dinner, so I am rarely the one who holds back in a discussion.

w1.2: We spent the whole first session on abbreviation conventions in funerary inscriptions. I had not appreciated how much of the reading is reconstruction rather than transcription.

w4.1: The joint sessions are timetabled at an hour that works for exactly one of the two groups, and it is not ours. Three people from our side have simply stopped coming and I do not blame them for it.

w4.2: Our joint transcription came back with the comment that we had used two different citation styles throughout. That is because we never agreed on one, and nobody wanted to be the person who insisted.

w7.1: I no longer think of the shared sessions as part of the seminar at all. We attend, we present, we leave. Whatever collaboration was supposed to happen is not happening, and at this point I would rather have the hour back.

w7.2: The palaeography exercises are excellent, though. Being handed a photograph with no transcription and told to argue for a reading is the most useful thing I have done in this degree.

w10.1: If the seminar runs again in this format I would not recommend it to anyone. The subject deserves better than a video link that drops out every ten minutes.

w10.2: I should also say that I am impatient with people who take a long time to answer, and I know that is partly a habit from my own upbringing rather than a fair standard to hold anyone to. I probably read hesitation as disinterest when it was nothing of the kind.
